What Makes Us Different

The Floor Rule

If any HUMAN dimension scores below 42, the composite is capped at 50 — no matter how high the others are.

Greatness in three areas doesn't excuse failure in a fourth.

This is why HI Grade refuses to average over moral failure — and why companies like Apple and Goldman Sachs can score 50 despite excellence in some dimensions. The threshold is 42 (a nod to The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy) because the bar should be memorable.

Most ESG ratings average across categories — environmental excellence can hide labor failures. HI Grade's floor rule is the mathematical enforcement of "balance over averaging."

For Journalists

Notable findings

Story angles already supported by the live data:

AI Displacement

Meta's H dimension was a leading indicator

Meta laid off ~21,000 employees across 2022–2023 (11,000 in November 2022, 10,000 more announced March 2023). In January 2025, on The Joe Rogan Experience, CEO Mark Zuckerberg announced that AI would replace mid-level engineers within the year. Our H dimension — built from SEC filings, BLS wages, USPTO patents, and pay-ratio disclosures — reflected the trajectory in our data months before each round hit the headlines.

The Bragging Cycle

Klarna captured the full AI bragging-and-rehiring loop

In 2024, Klarna's CEO publicly claimed AI was doing the work of 700 customer service representatives. In 2025, they quietly began hiring humans back. HI Grade's H dimension captured both moves — we score reversals and direction, not just snapshots.

The Open Disclosure

The N dimension is structurally compressed — and we say so

The Notable (N) dimension currently ranges 29–64 because three of its five sub-signals (audit quality, disclosure frequency, humanwashing detection) are spec'd but not yet wired in v1.2.1. This means companies cluster narrowly on N. We disclose this openly on the methodology page and in our README. Hiding it would be hypocritical for a transparency framework.

About the Build

How it was made

HI Grade was built by one person — a dad and systems engineer. That's enough about me.

The scoring engine uses zero AI; every score is reconstructable from raw public data, and the entire methodology is open source under Apache 2.0. The decision to keep AI out was deliberate: a framework that judges how companies treat humans has no business outsourcing those judgments to a machine.

Kind to humans and the earth, in that order.
